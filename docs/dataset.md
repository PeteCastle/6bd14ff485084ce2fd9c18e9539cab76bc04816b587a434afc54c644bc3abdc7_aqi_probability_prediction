# Dataset

This project uses air quality index (AQI) data sourced from the **OpenWeatherMap API**, covering 138 cities globally from 2023 to 2025. For consistency and regional focus, we limit the scope to cities within Metro Manila, Philippines. The dataset contains hourly measurements of seven key air pollutants: **sulfur dioxide (SO₂), nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), particulate matter (PM10 and PM2.5), ozone (O₃), and carbon monoxide (CO)**. These pollutants are commonly monitored in environmental health studies and serve as the prediction targets for our probabilistic forecasting models.

Sources:
- [2023 to 2024 Data (Kaggle)](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/bwandowando/philippine-major-cities-air-quality-data)
- [2025 Data (Kaggle)](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/bwandowando/philippine-cities-air-quality-index-data-2025/data)

### Preprocessing and Train Test Split

The preprocessing begins by collecting all raw air quality CSV files from each city, reading them one by one, and combining them into a single DataFrame. Only relevant columns are retained, and the data is filtered to include only the cities of interest. The records are sorted by city and timestamp, and the timestamps are standardized to UTC with hourly resolution. Negative pollutant readings are clamped to zero to eliminate invalid values. Each city’s data is then processed separately: timestamps are set as the index, duplicate hours are averaged, missing hourly intervals are filled in to create a continuous timeline, and gaps are interpolated linearly to maintain realistic temporal trends. Afterward, the datasets are concatenated across all cities, one-hot encoding is applied to the city name for machine learning compatibility, and the original city label is preserved for interpretability. The fully processed dataset is saved in parquet format for efficient reuse.

The train and validation split occurs later, during model training itself. Since this is a time series problem, the split is not random but instead respects temporal order to avoid leakage. Input samples are constructed as rolling windows of fixed length, defined by the lookback horizon, which specifies how many past hours of data are used as features. The target is the pollutant vector at a fixed point in the future, typically one day ahead. The stride hyperparameter controls how frequently windows are sampled along the timeline. The available sequence of windows is then partitioned chronologically into training and validation sets, with the validation portion taken from the tail of the series to mimic forward-in-time forecasting. This approach ensures that the model learns only from the past and is evaluated on unseen future intervals, and the effective train–test boundary shifts depending on the choice of lookback window and step size.
